The manual gives you a four-set Venn diagram. It is correct and it is unusable under time
pressure — you end up tracing overlaps with a finger while someone counts down.

It's really a 4x4 table: wire colouring down the side, LED and ★ across the top.

<ComplicatedWiresChart />

## Group by instruction, not by wire

Reading the chart row by row means memorizing sixteen cells. Grouping by the *answer* means
memorizing five short lists, and it matches how the question arrives in game — you're looking
at one wire and want one answer.

<CutGroups />

## How I hold each group

**Cut and Don't Cut** are mostly straight memorization, but there's one pairing that sticks:

> Red Star = **Cut**, Blue Star = **Don't cut**

Beyond that, make sure White and White Star are both cut. The Don't-Cuts matter less than
they look — if no rule applies you shouldn't be cutting anyway — but the memorable one is
**don't cut when everything is true** (LED Red Blue Star).

**Serial Even** covers wires 1-3: cut when the colour is not white (red, blue, or both) and
there is no LED and no star. The fourth is covered by the Red Blue rule below.

**Red Blue** is the one that needs its own thought. From Serial Even you already know to cut
a Red Blue wire on an even serial. If the **LED is on** with no star, still serial-even.
If there's a **star** with no LED, it becomes parallel port instead.

**Parallel Port and Batteries** are easiest learned together. Both require the **LED on**;
the star is optional either way. Then:

> Blue = **Parallel Port**, Red = **Batteries**

That covers wires 1-2 of each. Wire 3 of Parallel Port is already handled by the Red Blue
rule, and wire 3 of Batteries is a genuine exception you just have to know.

## In game

Call out the wire as three facts in a fixed order — colour, LED, star — before you look
anything up. "Red, no LED, star." Saying them in the same order every time is what stops you
from missing the star on the one wire where it flips the answer.
