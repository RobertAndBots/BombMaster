Rules refer to a button by one of four things, and telling them apart is most of this module:

- **P#** — position
- **L4** — the button *labelled* "4"
- **S#P** — the position you pressed at stage #
- **S#L** — the label you pressed at stage #

**D#** below means "the display shows #". Position and label are different lookups: the
buttons reshuffle every stage, so "the same label as stage 2" is usually in a different place
now.

## Memorizing the table

**Stage 1** is nearly free: displays 2, 3 and 4 map to the matching position. The only thing
to remember is **display 1 → position 2**.

**Stage 2** — displays 2 and 4 are the "easy" ones, because they both mean *the same position
as stage 1*, which means you only have one position to carry forward. For the other two,
remember **314**: display 3 → position 1, display 1 → label 4. Think of pi.

**Stage 3** — remember **3-3-3**: stage 3, display 3, position 3. Then display 4 → label 4.
The remaining two are the labels from stages 1 and 2, *switched*.

**Stage 4** is mostly positions borrowed from earlier stages. Everything before display 2 is
stage 1's position, everything after is stage 2's position, and display 2 → position 1.

**Stage 5** is only labels. Each display points at the stage whose label you want — except 3
and 4 are switched.

## What you actually carry in your head

This is the part the table doesn't tell you. During the module you need the **labels from
stages 1-4** and the **positions from stages 1-2**. I hold that as three two-digit numbers:

1. stage 1 position, stage 2 position
2. stage 1 label, stage 2 label
3. **stage 4** label, **stage 3** label

Note the third is deliberately backwards. Stage 5 maps display 3 → stage 4's label and
display 4 → stage 3's label, so storing them pre-swapped means I don't have to flip anything
under time pressure. You might find a flat four-digit number easier — experiment.

Two more things that cut the load:

- At **stage 4**, the moment you've chosen your button you can forget both positions entirely
  and hold only labels. Stage 5 never asks for a position.
- At **stage 2**, if the display is 2 or 4 then stages 1 and 2 share a position, so there is
  only one position to carry instead of two.

## Practice

The history is hidden by default below. That's the point — if you can see what you pressed,
you're drilling the table, and the table isn't what fails.
