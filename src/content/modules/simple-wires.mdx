There are five wire colours — **(B)** black, **(U)** blue, **(R)** red, **(W)** white,
**(Y)** yellow — and five kinds of condition:

- **0C** — no wires of colour C
- **1C** — exactly one wire of colour C
- **2C** — *two or more* wires of colour C
- **LC** — the last wire is colour C
- the serial number is odd

Note that **B is black and U is blue**. Every mistake I made early on was reading U as
"unknown" or B as "blue", and it costs you a strike every time.

## Split the cuts from the conditions

This is the whole trick for this module. The manual interleaves "if this, cut that", so it
reads like twenty separate rules. It isn't. You are only ever asked to cut a wire in a
specific position, or the last wire of a certain colour — so learn the *sequence of cuts*
for each wire count first, as a short number:

- **3 wires:** 23 blue 3
- **4 wires:** red 1142
- **5 wires:** 4121
- **6 wires:** 3464

The last cut in each group has no condition attached — it is the "otherwise" case.

Now the conditions. For 4, 5 and 6 wires, the **first** condition always also requires an odd
serial number, and no other condition ever mentions the serial. So that is one fact, not
three. The rest:

<div class="scroll-x">

| 3 wires | 4 wires | 5 wires | 6 wires |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0R | 2R | LB | 0Y |
| LW | LY 0R | 1R 2Y | 1Y 2W |
| 2U | 1U | 0B | 0R |
|  | 2Y | | |

</div>

Read a column top to bottom against the cut sequence for that count and the module falls out.
Six wires, for instance: *no yellow and odd serial → third*, *one yellow and two-plus white →
fourth*, *no red → sixth*, otherwise *fourth*.

## The 4-wire shortcut

Conditions 2 and 3 for four wires both cut the **first** wire, so you can merge them:

- **2R** → cut the last red
- **LY 0R** *or* **1U** → cut the first
- **2Y** → cut the fourth
- otherwise → cut the second

Which shortens the cut sequence to **red 142**. Fewer things to hold.

## What I kept getting wrong

**2C means two _or more_.** For a long time I read "2Y" on the six-wire column as "exactly
two yellow" and cut the wrong wire on any module with three. The manual says *more than one*.

## Practice

The drill below does not pick a random bomb and then solve it — it picks which condition
should fire and builds a module that satisfies that one and defeats every condition above
it. That matters, because random wires would give you the common cases over and over and you
would never drill the rules at the bottom of each column.
