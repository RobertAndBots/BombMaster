The pieces: five button colours (black, blue, red, white, yellow), four labels (Abort,
Detonate, Hold, Press) and four strip colours (blue, red, white, yellow).

As with [Simple Wires](/modules/simple-wires/), memorize the **actions** and the
**conditions** separately.

## Actions

The actions alternate, starting with hold:

> hold, press, hold, press, hold, press, hold

That's it. That's the entire action table. If you know which condition fired, you know
whether to hold by whether its number is odd.

## Conditions

1. Blue, Abort
2. 2+ batteries, Detonate
3. White, CAR
4. 3+ batteries, FRK
5. Yellow
6. Red, Hold

The mnemonic I use is just the list said out loud:

> **"Blue abort, 2 det, white car, 3 FRK, yellow, red hold"**

After enough repetitions you won't need a hook for it, but early on I pictured a car blowing
up for "det white car", and remembered that after **2** you skip a number and land on **3**.
And **FRK is three letters**, which pins it to the 3.

## Releasing

If you're holding, a strip lights up and you release when the timer shows a particular digit
in any position:

- **Blue = 4**
- **Yellow = 5**
- **Everything else = 1**

Two facts and a default.

## The shortcut worth having

Conditions 2, 4 and 6 are the press-and-release ones, and two of the three need batteries.
So: **if the bomb has fewer than two batteries, the only thing you have to check is whether
the button is a red "Hold".** If it is, press and release. If not, hold. You can skip the
entire table.

Glance at the batteries first and most modules resolve before you've read the label.

## Practice

The batteries and indicators start hidden below, and that's deliberate — on a real bomb they
are on a different face, so you have to know you need them before you go looking. A drill
that showed you everything up front would train the wrong reflex.
