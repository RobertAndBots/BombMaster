Nine rows per colour, and occurrences are **cumulative across every panel** in the module —
so the real difficulty isn't the table, it's keeping three running counts while panels scroll
past.

<WireSequenceTable />

## Observations that hold the table together

Three facts cut the memorization down a lot:

- **Row 5 is B for every colour.** One row, free.
- **Blue and black share rows 2 and 3. Red and black share rows 4 and 5.** Black is the
  bridge between the other two.
- **The last row differs for each colour: B, A, C.** A clean anchor at the bottom.

## Counting between panels

This is the part that actually decides whether you solve the module. I keep three numbers —
occurrences of red, blue, black, always in that order — and **say them out loud after every
panel**, while the next one is loading.

So if the first panel shows blue, red, blue and you click next, say "**1, 2, 0**" into the
dead time. It feels silly. It is also the difference between solving this module and
restarting your count three panels in, because there is nothing on screen that tells you what
you've already seen.

Say them even when a panel had no wires. The rhythm is what keeps the count alive.
